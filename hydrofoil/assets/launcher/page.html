How the flight controllers work (Auto Combined vs LQR vs PID)
Auto Combined (Recommended) Uses PID while resting in the water, accelerating through the hull drag hump, and slowing down to land. Once foilborne and steady, it fades smoothly (0.5 s blend) into LQR for smooth waves and banked turns — and automatically fades back to PID when slowing down or touching the water.
PID (Three Independent Loops) Simple beginner-friendly loop trio: height error moves both front flaps together, pitch error tilts the back wing, and roll error splits the front flaps left vs right. Reliable when hull-wetted, though it doesn't coordinate wing-pitch coupling or turn slip cleanly.
LQR (Coordinated Foilborne Brain) Knows all physics couplings together (lifting front flaps pitches the nose, banking induces sideways slip and adverse yaw, motors have delay) and schedules gains continuously with water speed. Handles waves and banked turns tightly once airborne.

    Boat editor & balance calculator

    Adjust wing dimensions, boat weight, and where items sit on deck. Drag weights directly on the blueprint below (or type numbers on the left) and the Center of Gravity (CG), rotational inertias, trim curve, and plain-English assessment update live before you fly.

    Physics note: wings use rectangular planform NACA 0009 section polars (span, chord, aspect ratio, flap fraction, and incidence scale the model). The CAD boat's flaps sit next to the centreline, and their extra lift is applied there, so roll control is modelled with the real (short) lever arm. Adding weights computes full 3D Center of Gravity (forward x, starboard y, vertical z) and 3D rotational inertias via the parallel-axis theorem; moving CG vertically updates roll/pitch inertias and is reported in the balance readout.

    Autotuner

    Tests candidates across every flight phase — Rising (takeoff & supervised hump climb), Cruising & Turning (head waves, following waves, rider weight shift, noisy real-world sensors, and 12° banked 3D turns), and Falling (descent, touchdown & LQR → PID handback). The default Both (Phase-Aware) mode optimizes PID gains specifically for rising/falling while optimizing LQR weights specifically for foilborne cruising and 3D turns. You can switch tabs freely while tuning runs in the background — results auto-save to local/overrides.json.

    Methods and sources

    PID: a relay feedback test in the sim on each loop (Åström and Hägglund, Automatica 20(5), 1984) gives the ultimate gain and period, then the Ziegler–Nichols rules (Trans. ASME 64, 1942) and Tyreus–Luyben (Ind. Eng. Chem. Res. 31(11), 1992). Skogestad's SIMC rules (J. Process Control 13, 2003) need a step-response model, which the open-loop unstable heave loop cannot give, so they are not used.

    LQR: Bryson's rule (Bryson and Ho, Applied Optimal Control, 1975), then an iterative re-weighting that tightens whatever overshot its target. That loop is this project's own heuristic, not a published method.

    Refinement: a cross-entropy search (Rubinstein, Methodology and Computing in Applied Probability 1, 1999) started from the best of the above.
